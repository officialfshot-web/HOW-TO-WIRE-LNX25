    Behind the door

    Inside settings cheat sheet

    SettingWhat it doesStarting point / tip
    Pressure armHow hard the drive rolls squeeze the wire. It has a number scale.Lincoln's manual: solid steel or stainless 4–6, cored (flux-core) 3–4. Cored wire is softer, so less squeeze.
    Spindle brakeDrag on the spool.Just enough that the spool stops when the wire stops. Like the drag on a reel.
    2-step / 4-step switchTrigger mode.2-step for tacks and short welds. 4-step for long beads.
    Cold feed / gas purgeFeeds wire or flows gas without turning on weld power.Cold feed when loading wire. Purge when setting gas flow.
    Run-in speedHow fast the wire creeps in before the arc lights.Slower run-in gives softer starts.
    Pre-flow / post-flowGas before the arc starts and after it stops.Post-flow covers the hot crater so it doesn't get porous.
    BurnbackKeeps the arc on a split second after the wire stops so the wire doesn't stick in the puddle.Set too long, it melts the wire back into the tip. Getting a ball welded on the tip? Have whoever sets up the machine check this.
    Thermal lightThe drive motor got too hot. It shuts the drive off for up to about 30 seconds to cool.If it keeps happening, look for drag: dirty liner, kinked cable, tension too high.
    Trigger at power-upIf you're holding the trigger when the feeder powers up, it won't feed.Let go of the trigger, then pull again.

    Run-in, pre-flow, post-flow, and burnback are set through the setup menu on most units. Check your manual or your lead before changing them.

    Inside the case

    Feeder parts, spool to gun

    Spool & spindleHolds the wire. The spindle brake keeps the spool from free-spinning when you let off the trigger. Retainer locks it on.
    Inlet guideThe tube the wire enters before it reaches the drive rolls.
    Drive rollsGrooved wheels that push the wire. Size is stamped on the roll. V-groove for solid, knurled for flux-core.
    Pressure armTension knob that squeezes the rolls onto the wire. Too loose slips, too tight crushes.
    Gun bushingWhere the gun plugs in. Push it all the way in and lock it, or wire and gas feed badly.
    Cold feedFeeds wire with no power and no gas. Use it to load wire.
    Gas purgeFlows gas with no wire so you can set flow and clear the line.
    2-step / 4-step2-step: hold trigger to weld. 4-step: click on, click off.
    LinerTube inside the gun cable that the wire rides through. Dirty or kinked liners cause stutter and burnback.

    Remember

    Angles & motion

    Holding the gun

    If there's slag, you dragFlux-core leaves slag, so drag it like stick. Solid wire with gas, push it.
    Bacon, not popcornA good wire arc sizzles steady. Pops and crackles mean your settings are off.
    Clip, then weldSnip the ball off the wire before every start.
    Firm handshakeRoll tension: tight enough that it doesn't slip, not so tight it crushes.

    Stick habits hold the arc too tight and drag everything. Set the process, then move the sliders to see how the angle and distance should look.

    Cursive "e" motion

    How to run it

    • Loops about the width of the bead, 1/4" or less.
    • Pause a split second on each toe so it wets in. The dots show where.
    • Move quicker through the middle so it doesn't pile up.
    • Keep the rhythm even. Consistent spacing matters more than loop size.
    Work angle: about 45° into a fillet, 90° on a flat butt, slightly up toward the top plate on horizontal.

    Remember it

    Do it in your head

    One amp per thouEvery 0.001" of steel takes about 1 amp. 1/8" is 0.125", so about 125 amps.
    .045 is one-for-oneWith .045 wire, wire speed in inches per minute is about the same number as your amps.
    Skinny wire, fast feed.035 × 1.6, .030 × 2, .023 × 3.5. Thinner wire has to move faster to carry the same amps.
    Floor, ceiling, middleDrop volts until the wire stubs. Raise them until the arc goes wild. Weld halfway between.
